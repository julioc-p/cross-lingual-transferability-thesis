\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Abstract.}

This work investigates the impact of continual multilingual pretraining on an LLM's cross-lingual transferability and performance for text-to-SPARQL generation, focusing on the source languages English and German and the knowledge graph Wikidata.
It compares Occiglot-7b-eu5, a model resulting from continually pretraining Mistral 7B v0.1 on five major European languages (English, Spanish, German, Italian, French) \cite{avramidis-etal-2024-occiglot}, against the base Mistral model.
A key contribution is the development of a large-scale, reproducible multilingual dataset (895,954 examples) with 12 different languages derived from QALD \cite{usbeck2024qald} and other knowledge extraction challenges \cite{jiang2022knowledge}, augmented with German translations and Wikidata entity/relationship mappings; this dataset has been made publicly available.
Experiments fine-tuning eight model variants (two versions of each model for both languages, with and without contextual aid -- v1 and v2 experiments respectively) were conducted.

The findings indicate that continual multilingual pre-training significantly benefits Occiglot's performance in German when contextual aid is absent (v1).
However, providing German entity/relationship mappings to Mistral (v2) substantially reduces this performance gap.
Conversely, for English on the QALD-10 test set \cite{usbeck2024qald}, while Occiglot maintained comparable executable query rates, Mistral outperformed Occiglot in both finetuned v1 and v2 models in terms of F1 score.
This suggests a potential trade-off where multilingual adaptation might diminish the capability of handling nuance in the original language for Occiglot.

A primary bottleneck identified in v1 models is entity/relationship linking, with performance increasing approximately fourfold when contextual mappings are supplied (v2). 
Interestingly, this improvement was less pronounced on the QALD-10 test set, suggesting that its inherent query complexity also poses a second significant challenge, largely not present in the other test sets.
Nevertheless, by incorporating contextual mappings, competitive F1-scores were achieved on QALD-10 (around 0.25), comparable to systems in the QALD-10 benchmark (0.1-0.5 F1) \cite{usbeck2024qald}.
This result also suggests that direct pre-training on Wikidata entity/relationship identifiers, a hypothesis considered, might yield similar outcomes.
Finally, finetuned Occiglot generally demonstrated slightly superior percentage of executable query generation in English in comparison to fine-tuned Mistral, hinting at benefits of multilingual exposure for correct syntax generation.
\end{abstract}
